# Sleep is a skill

Every other note in this hub runs on top of this one. Sleep debt degrades
attention, mood, impulse control and training quality — and it does so in a way
that makes you feel like the problem is you.

Treat sleep as a **skill with a protocol**, not a nightly negotiation.

## Two systems

| System | What it does | What wrecks it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sleep pressure** | Builds while awake; adenosine accumulates | Naps late in the day, too much caffeine |
| **Circadian rhythm** | Timing signal for when sleep is possible | Light at the wrong time, late meals, irregular wake times |

Your goal is boring: build pressure during the day, and land the timing signal at
the same time every night.

## The basics, in priority order

1. **Fixed wake time.** Same time every day, weekends included (±30 min). This anchors everything else.
2. **Morning light.** Go outside within an hour of waking, ten to twenty minutes. Indoor light is not a substitute.
3. **Caffeine curfew.** Roughly eight to ten hours before bed for most people — caffeine's half-life is longer than the "I can sleep fine" feeling suggests.
4. **Dim the evening.** Lower lights after sunset, screens down or dimmed, and a defined last-look-at-phone time.
5. **Cool, dark, quiet room.** Temperature down, blackout if possible, earplugs if not.
6. **Consistent wind-down.** Twenty minutes of the same low-stimulation thing: shower, stretches, paper book.

:::tip[The best sleep tracker is a bedtime]

Wearables are interesting and frequently wrong about stages. They're useful for
one thing: trend data on sleep duration and consistency. Don't let a readiness
score run your day.

:::

## The 10-minute failure protocol

For the nights it doesn't work, have a plan that isn't "lie there getting angry":

- **Don't watch the clock.** Turn it away.
- **Get up after ~20 minutes** of clear wakefulness. Dim light, boring book, no phone.
- **Return when sleepy**, not when bored.
- **Keep the wake time** the next morning. Sleeping in to "catch up" moves the problem to tomorrow night.
- **Write the worry down.** Most nocturnal thinking is a loop looking for a container.

## Signs the system is off

- Falling asleep within seconds of hitting the pillow (likely cumulative debt).
- Waking at 3 a.m. with a racing mind (often stress or alcohol).
- Afternoon collapse that requires caffeine to survive.
- Needing an alarm to feel human, every single day.

If several of these are persistent, that's information — and worth raising with a
doctor rather than solving with a new productivity app.

## The minimum protocol

When everything is chaotic, keep three things:

1. Same wake time.
2. Light in the morning, dim in the evening.
3. Phone out of the bedroom.

That alone does more than most sleep supplements.
