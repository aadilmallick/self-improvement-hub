# Training & movement

The point of training here is not aesthetics. It is **capacity**: the energy to
work, think, and stay even-tempered. That changes the prescription — consistency
beats intensity, and the best program is the one you don't dread.

## The minimum effective week

| Component | Dose | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strength** | 2–3 sessions, 30–45 min | Protects joints, muscle, and metabolic health |
| **Zone 2 cardio** | 2–3 sessions, 25–40 min, conversational pace | Builds the base that makes everything else easier |
| **Movement snacks** | Daily walking, 20–60 min total | The most underrated energy lever |
| **Mobility / skill** | 2–3 short sessions | Keeps the body usable as you age |

This is deliberately modest. If you already train more, you don't need this note.

## A week that actually fits

```
Mon  Strength A (lower focus) 35 min
Tue  Zone 2 — walk or bike 30 min
Wed  Strength B (upper focus) 35 min
Thu  Zone 2 — 30 min + 10 min mobility
Fri  Strength A or skill work 30 min
Sat  Long walk / hike / sport
Sun  Rest or easy walk
```

Two of the three strength sessions can happen at home with a pair of dumbbells
and a floor. Fancy equipment is a hobby, not a requirement.

:::tip[The 10-minute rule]

On a low day, do ten minutes and stop if you still want to. Starting is the
skill; the session is a bonus. Ten honest minutes, three times a week, beats one
heroic Saturday.

:::

## Progressive overload, simply

Pick a small number of movements and add a little each week:

- **Lower:** squat or split squat, hinge (deadlift / hip thrust), carry.
- **Upper:** push (press / push-up), pull (row / chin-up).
- **Core:** one anti-extension or anti-rotation movement.

Add reps until the top of a range, then add load, then reset reps. That's it.
No periodization spreadsheet required.

## Recovery is part of the program

- Sleep first. Training on five hours of sleep builds fatigue, not capacity.
- Eat enough protein and enough total food. Chronic under-eating is a training problem disguised as discipline.
- Take a deload week roughly every eight weeks, or whenever motivation disappears simultaneously with progress.
- Pain that changes your movement pattern is a signal to see a professional, not to push through.

## Energy budgeting

Training and work compete for the same body. Two habits prevent the collision:

1. **Train before the day's hardest cognitive block** if your schedule allows — the post-session clarity is real.
2. **Don't stack a hard session onto a bad-sleep day.** Swap it for a walk. This is not weakness; it is load management.

## Tracking

Three lines per week in your review:

```
Strength sessions: _ / 2
Zone 2 / walks: _ / _
Energy (1–5 avg):
```

Nothing else. If you want detail, log the working weights — never the mood,
unless the mood is the actual metric.
