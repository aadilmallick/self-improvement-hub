# Motivation is a lagging indicator

Motivation is usually described as the fuel for action. In practice it is closer
to the **receipt**: it tends to show up after you have started, not before.

## The order of operations

```
action → evidence → mood
```

You do a small thing, you get evidence that you are the kind of person who does
it, and *then* the mood arrives. Waiting for the mood first is asking the receipt
to print before the transaction.

This is why the first five minutes decide the day, and why "I'll do it when I
feel ready" is a plan to never start.

## The three things that actually move behaviour

| Lever | What it means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Clarity** | You know exactly what the next physical action is | "Open the doc and write the worst paragraph" |
| **Ability** | The action is possible in your current state | "If exhausted, do the 5-minute version" |
| **Permission** | You have decided the cost is acceptable | "This will be mediocre and that's allowed" |

Ambition is not on the list. Ambition is a multiplier applied after the action
exists.

:::tip[The two-minute entry]

Define an entry action so small it cannot fail: open the file, put on the shoes,
write one sentence. The entry is the commitment. The rest is momentum.

:::

## The minimum viable day

Every plan needs a bad-day version, or the bad day becomes the end of the plan.

Write three columns:

| Mode | When | What counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full** | Normal energy | The real session |
| **Minimum** | Tired, busy | 10 minutes, one page, one set |
| **Recovery** | Sick, overwhelmed | Log it, sleep, resume tomorrow |

The minimum is not a moral compromise; it is the thing that keeps the identity
intact. A minimum day still casts a vote.

## Obstacles, pre-committed

Before the week starts, name the two most likely obstacles and the response you
will use. This is a tiny amount of work that removes a surprising amount of
in-the-moment negotiation.

```
If I am too tired after dinner → I do the 10-minute version at 21:00.
If I miss a morning session → I do not double up; I resume the next scheduled day.
If I travel → I pack the minimum kit and count the walk as the session.
```

## When motivation is genuinely absent

Sometimes the problem is not discipline, it is depletion:

- **Sleep debt** masquerading as laziness.
- **Unclear goals** creating low-grade anxiety instead of drive.
- **Boredom** from doing the same thing for too long.
- **Depression or burnout** — which is a clinical issue, not a productivity issue. Rest, and get help if it lasts.

Before you redesign your system, check the boring causes first.
