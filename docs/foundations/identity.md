# Identity-based change

Most people try to change their **outcomes** first: lose ten pounds, read more,
wake up earlier. That order is backwards and it leaks willpower. This note is
about the order that actually holds.

## The three layers

```
identity  →  process  →  outcome
(who)        (what)       (result)
```

You can push outcomes directly — with deadlines and shame — and it works for
about three weeks. You can build processes without identity and they collapse
the first time they get inconvenient. Start at the top and the lower layers
stop needing supervision.

| Layer | Example | Fails when… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | "Run a 22-minute 5K" | It is the only thing you track |
| Process | "Run three times a week, Tue/Thu/Sun" | It has no owner |
| Identity | "I am someone who trains" | It is a slogan with no evidence |

## Vote, don't announce

An identity is not a decision, it is a **record of evidence**. Every action that
is consistent with it is a vote. You do not need unanimity — you need a majority
and a short memory for the abstentions.

The practical version:

1. Name the identity in a sentence you would say out loud without cringing.
2. Find the **smallest visible action** that a person with that identity would obviously take.
3. Take it today, badly, and log it.
4. Never let two consecutive votes go missing.

:::tip[Shrink the vote until it is embarrassing]

"I am someone who writes" does not require 1,000 words. It requires opening the
document and writing a sentence. The vote is about **casting it**, not about it
being impressive.

:::

## The inversion: be → do → have

The usual plan is *have → do → be*: I will have more time, then I will do the
reading, then I will be a reader. It never arrives.

Flip it:

- **Be** — decide the identity now. Today. For free.
- **Do** — behave like that person at the scale you can currently afford.
- **Have** — outcomes accumulate as a side effect.

## Standards vs. goals

| Goals | Standards |
| --- | --- |
| Have an end date | Have no end date |
| Measured against a target | Measured against a floor |
| Motivating until you miss | Deciding, not motivating |
| "I want to read 30 books" | "I read for 20 minutes before bed" |

Goals tell you the direction. Standards tell you what happens on an ordinary
Tuesday. You need a handful of goals and a very small number of standards.

## An identity audit (20 minutes)

Write these out. Paper is better than a document for this one.

1. **What do I claim to value?** List five things.
2. **What does my calendar say I value?** Look at the last two weeks honestly.
3. **Where is the gap widest?** Not where it is most shameful — where it is widest.
4. **Pick one identity** that closes part of that gap. Just one.
5. **Define the smallest daily vote** and where it goes in the day.
6. **Name the story that will try to stop it.** ("I'm not a morning person.") Write the counter-evidence.

:::warning[Watch for borrowed identities]

"A founder", "a disciplined person", "a morning person" may be someone else's
identity that you have been renting. If you cannot describe what a Tuesday looks
like for that person, and whether you would enjoy it, it is probably borrowed.

:::
