# How habits actually form

A habit is not a personality trait, a moral victory, or a test of will. It is a
**compressed decision**: a cue that used to require thought, now firing without
it. This note is the mechanics plus the failure modes I keep running into.

## The loop

| Stage | What it is | If you want to build it | If you want to break it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cue** | The trigger | Make it obvious | Make it invisible |
| **Craving** | The pull toward relief | Make it attractive | Make it unappealing |
| **Response** | The behaviour | Make it easy | Make it hard |
| **Reward** | The payoff | Make it satisfying | Make it unsatisfying |

:::note[The honest part]

The craving is rarely about the behaviour. It is about the *state change*: from
bored to stimulated, from anxious to soothed, from alone to connected. If you
delete a habit without replacing the state change, the craving will find a
different door.

:::

## Practical levers, in order of power

### 1. Make it easy (first, always)

- **Two-minute version:** shrink the behaviour until starting is trivial. Read one page. Put on shoes. Open the file.
- **Reduce steps:** lay out clothes the night before, keep the guitar out of the case, pre-fill the water bottle.
- **Prime the environment:** the best habit app is a physical object in the wrong place.

### 2. Anchor it to something that already happens

Habit stacking: `After I [existing habit], I will [new habit].`

```
After I pour my morning coffee, I will write tomorrow's top task.
After I close my laptop at 18:00, I will walk for ten minutes.
After I get into bed, I will read one page of fiction.
```

Anchor to something with a **reliable time**, not a mood.

### 3. Make the cue visible

- A tracker on the wall, not buried in an app.
- Same time, same place, same order.
- One reminder, not four. Multiple alarms train you to dismiss alarms.

### 4. Attach a reward that is not a screen

The reward can be the tick itself if the tracker is visible enough. If you need
more, use something immediate and small: a good coffee, ten minutes of music, a
genuine "done" note in the log. Don't reward habits with the exact stimulation
you are trying to reduce.

## Failure modes

:::warning[Missing twice]

One missed day is an accident. Two is the start of a new pattern. The rule is
not "never miss" — it is **never miss twice**. On the second miss, cut the habit
to its two-minute version rather than abandoning it.

:::

:::warning[Habit stacking queues]

Five habits chained together is a fragile domino run. Chain at most **two**, and
make the chain independent of each other.

:::

:::warning[Tracking the wrong thing]

Tracking "days I felt productive" gives you nothing to act on. Track the
**behaviour**, not the verdict: sessions completed, pages read, bedtime hit.

:::

:::warning[Confusing ambition with scope]

"I will go to the gym for 90 minutes four times a week" is a plan for someone who
already has the habit. Start with two sessions of 25 minutes and let the identity
do the rest.

:::

## A habit audit

Copy this table into your notes and fill it in monthly.

| Habit | Keeps / Drains | Cue | Friction (1–5) | Keep, shrink, or kill |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Morning phone scroll | Drains | Waking up | 1 | Make harder |
| 20-min walk | Keeps | After lunch | 2 | Keep |
| Evening reading | Keeps | After teeth | 3 | Shrink |

The goal is not a perfect table. The goal is to stop carrying habits you never
chose.
