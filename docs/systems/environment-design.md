# Environment design

Discipline is expensive. Environments are cheap. Every habit you are fighting
is being decided by a room, a phone, and a set of defaults — usually without
your involvement.

## Two levers

| Lever | Question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Friction** | How many steps until the behaviour? | Phone in another room = +1 step, -80% of use |
| **Cues** | Is the reminder visible? | Guitar on a stand, water bottle on the desk |

Add friction to what you want less of. Remove friction from what you want more
of. That is the entire method.

## The phone is the main event

Most attention problems are phone problems, and most phone problems are
**presence** problems.

The stack, from least to most aggressive:

1. **Grayscale mode** — colour is a reward signal. Removing it makes the device visibly less interesting.
2. **Home screen with one page only** — no feeds, no browsers, no shopping. Keep tools: maps, notes, camera.
3. **Notifications off by default** — everything except calls from actual humans and calendar alerts.
4. **Charging outside the bedroom** — an alarm clock costs less than the sleep you're losing.
5. **Social apps logged out** — the login screen is a surprisingly effective speed bump.
6. **App blockers during work blocks** — decide at 09:00 and let the software hold the line at 11:00.

:::warning[Face-down phone is not absence]

A face-down phone on the desk still occupies attention — the effort of not
checking is a real cost. Distance beats orientation.

:::

## The desk

- **One surface, one job.** Where possible, separate work and rest spatially.
- **Visible next action.** A sticky note with the first task beats a full to-do list.
- **Nothing that glows** unless it's the thing you're working on.
- **A landing pad** for the debris of pockets and hands, so the desk doesn't become a shelf.

## Defaults beat decisions

- **Food:** what is at eye level in the fridge gets eaten. Prep the good option.
- **Training:** kit packed the night before, route decided, session on the calendar.
- **Reading:** book on the pillow, phone charging elsewhere.
- **Sleep:** lights dimmed, screens off at a set time, not when you feel finished.

## Social environment

The people around you set the invisible baseline.

- **Be explicit about your standards** — not as a lecture, as information. "I'm off screens after ten" is enough.
- **Find one person to report to weekly.** Social accountability works best with specificity, not encouragement.
- **Audit your feeds.** If a feed reliably makes you feel behind, it is not information, it is weather.

## A 30-minute reset

1. Clear the desk completely. Put back only what the next task needs.
2. Remove one app from the phone home screen (move it into a folder two taps deep).
3. Turn off all non-human notifications.
4. Put the phone charger somewhere that is not beside the bed.
5. Choose one object to place in plain sight that represents a habit you want.

That is it. No new system, no subscription.
