# Goals, quarters, and a 12-week year

Annual goals are mostly fiction: twelve months is far enough away to feel
optional and far enough from feedback to stay unexamined. **Twelve weeks** is
short enough to stay urgent and long enough to see real change.

## One metric per quarter

Choose **one** outcome metric and **two** leading behaviours. That is the whole
planning document.

| Type | Example | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | Ship the first version of the project | Direction |
| Leading 1 | 5 deep-work blocks per week | Predicts the outcome |
| Leading 2 | 3 training sessions per week | Protects capacity |

If you can't name a leading behaviour, the goal is a wish.

:::tip[Define the finish line in observable terms]

"Get fit" cannot be reviewed. "Run 5K without stopping by week 12" can. If a
stranger couldn't tell whether you hit it, it isn't a goal — it's a theme.

:::

## The quarter, week by week

- **Weeks 1–2:** build the behaviour. Results will be embarrassing. Ignore them.
- **Weeks 3–6:** raise difficulty slowly. This is where most plans overreach.
- **Weeks 7–10:** hold the line. Life will interfere; protect the minimum version.
- **Weeks 11–12:** finish, measure, and write a one-page retro.

Rest weeks are not a failure of the plan; they are part of it. Schedule one.

## The retro (one page)

```markdown
## Quarter retro — ____

Outcome metric: start → end
Leading behaviours hit: __ / 12 weeks

What worked:
What I avoided:
What I would do differently:
Keep / Change / Drop:
```

## Kill criteria

Write these **before** you start, while you are calm and optimistic:

- If after **four weeks** the leading behaviour is under 50%, shrink the goal — don't add willpower.
- If the goal no longer matters but the habit does, keep the habit and drop the goal.
- If it is causing consistent sleep loss, it is a net negative. Cut it.

That last one is not a joke. A lot of goals are just anxiety with a deadline.

## When to break the plan

Plans are for ordinary weeks. Some weeks are not ordinary: illness, grief,
travel, a launch. In those weeks, drop to the [minimum viable day](../foundations/motivation#the-minimum-viable-day)
and resume. The system is supposed to bend; that is what makes it durable.

## A quarterly reset (90 minutes)

1. **Review the retro** from the last quarter. Read the "keep / change / drop".
2. **Pick one outcome** for the next quarter. Write the observable finish line.
3. **Pick two leading behaviours.** Check they fit your real calendar, not your ideal one.
4. **Put them on the calendar** as recurring blocks before you leave the room.
5. **Write the kill criteria.**
6. **Delete one commitment** that no longer earns its place.
