When I feel unmotivated, my instinct is to look for a better plan. Almost every
time, the real problem is a **flattened baseline** — too much high-stimulation
input, and now everything ordinary feels like a tax.

Here's the audit I run before I change anything else.

{/* truncate */}

## The seven questions

1. What did I touch first this morning, before I was fully awake?
2. How many apps did I open today without deciding to?
3. When was the last time I was bored for thirty minutes without fixing it?
4. What am I avoiding when I reach for the phone? (Answer with a feeling, not a task.)
5. Did anything today produce a reward I had to work for?
6. How many hours of sleep did I get, honestly, averaged over the week?
7. What did I do yesterday that I'd be happy to repeat for a year?

The answers are almost never a surprise, which is the point. The audit isn't
diagnosis, it's **acknowledgement** — and acknowledgement is what makes the next
step feel obvious rather than imposed.

## Wanting vs. liking, in one paragraph

Dopamine is not the pleasure chemical. It is the anticipation system: the pull
toward something before you have it. Liking — the actual enjoyment — runs on
different chemistry. That gap explains the modern signature experience of craving
something intensely and feeling flat the moment it arrives. The want fired. The
like didn't.

## The two-week protocol

The full version is in [Focus & dopamine](/docs/foundations/focus-and-dopamine),
and there's an interactive walkthrough at
[Dopamine 101](/html/dopamine-walkthrough). The short version:

- **Morning:** no feed or notifications for the first 30 minutes.
- **Work:** phone in another room during a deep work block, not face-down on the desk.
- **Feed fast:** 14 days, scope written down before starting.
- **Craving:** ten minutes of [urge surfing](/docs/foundations/focus-and-dopamine#the-reset-protocols) instead of a decision.
- **Replacement:** a low-stimulation activity ready for the bored moment — walk, book, cooking.
- **Sleep:** phone out of the bedroom, same wake time.

:::warning[Days three to five are the worst]

The dip is the protocol working, not the protocol failing. Expect it, write down
what you expect to feel, and don't judge the experiment from inside the dip.

:::

## What to expect

Roughly two to four weeks before ordinary rewards start registering properly
again. Not transcendence — just the return of "reading a book is interesting" and
"this task isn't actually painful". That's the whole prize, and it's a large one.

## Why I wrote it down

Because in the middle of the dip I will not remember any of this. I'll remember
that I feel flat and that the obvious explanation is "this isn't working". The
audit is a note to the version of me who has already forgotten the argument.
